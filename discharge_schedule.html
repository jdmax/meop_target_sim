Time to reach the goal

Why switching helps, and how to measure fd

One polarization, set by MEOP

The target holds about Nt/Np times as many atoms as the pumping cell, and the exchange turns the pumping cell over in Np/G, far faster than MEOP builds it up. The two cells then share nearly one polarization P, and

N dP/dt = Np fd(P) − Nt P/T1t, fd(P) = (Mss,d − P)/Tb,d

where fd is the net production per pumping-cell atom with discharge level d, straight from the sealed-cell build-up.

Run whichever level is faster at the current P

The time to reach a goal is t = ∫ dP / (dP/dt). The integrand depends only on P, and the level can be chosen afresh at every P, so the time is shortest when every step dP is taken at the larger rate: run the level with the larger fd(P). The best schedule follows the upper envelope of the fd lines, switching where they cross.

The crossover doesn't depend on the losses

Target relaxation, and any other loss that depends only on P (wall relaxation, or a leak up the supply line if the valve were open), subtracts the same amount at a given P whichever level is running. Comparing levels at the same P compares fd alone, so the crossover

P* = (MH/TH − ML/TL) / (1/TH − 1/TL)

is fixed by the sealed-cell values, whatever the target T1, cell volumes or tubes. Written with the whole-system rates, dP/dt = kd(Pd − P), it is P* = (kHPH − kLPL)/(kH − kL): the losses change each kd and Pd, but not this combination. The levels table lists kd and Pd. The losses do decide how far up the envelope the build-up gets, and so whether a crossover is ever reached.

When a stronger discharge stops helping

All of this assumes the exchange keeps up, Np/(TbG) ≪ 1. As it approaches 1, the pumping cell runs ahead of the target, Pp and Pt separate, and a stronger discharge raises Pp without filling the target any faster; the schedule still switches on Pp, which is no longer the right variable. The page warns above 0.2. The full two-cell equations are solved throughout, so the times shown include this effect.

T1p splits fd but doesn't change it

A sealed-cell build-up already includes the relaxation the discharge causes: fd(P) = Γop(Plim − P) − P/T1p, with Γop + 1/T1p = 1/Tb and ΓopPlim = Mss/Tb. T1p shares out the same straight line between pumping and relaxation, so it sets Γop and Plim in the table but leaves the schedule unchanged.

Measuring fd(P)

  1. At each discharge level, run a sealed-cell build-up from zero at the operating pressure, field and laser power, and record M(t) with the optical polarimeter.
  2. Differentiate: fd(M) = dM/dt, plotted against M. Take the slope of a smoothed curve (a Savitzky–Golay filter, say) rather than raw differences, which the polarimeter noise swamps.
  3. A straight line from Mss/Tb at M = 0 to zero at M = Mss confirms the sealed-cell description, and its two ends are the table's inputs. Curvature (relaxation that grows with M, or the cell warming under a strong discharge) means the crossover should be read where the measured curves cross, not from Mss and Tb.
  4. As a direct check, switch the discharge level partway through one build-up at a known M: dM/dt just before and after the switch gives both fd(M) at the same M, and which is larger says which side of P* you are on.
